# Out-of-Stock Substitution Guide

> Marvel CPG is a fictional company created for the University at Buffalo CDA 650 capstone. This document was written by Team 1 for the project's RAG knowledge base. Figures marked *data* come from the project dataset; everything else is fictional business content.

## 1 Purpose

When a Marvel SKU is out of stock in a store, the shopper either buys another Marvel product, buys a competitor, goes to another store, waits, or does not buy at all. This guide tells field representatives and store staff which Marvel SKU to offer instead, in what order, and when there is no acceptable substitute. A substitute protects today's sale; it does not fix the stock-out. Always also fix the cause (section 8).

## 2 Substitution rules

Offer a substitute in this order of preference:

1. **Same product, different pack size.** The same formula in a smaller or larger pack (for example Laundry Pods 20ct for Laundry Pods 42ct). This is the closest match for the shopper.
2. **Same product line, different scent or variant.** The same use and pack in another fragrance (for example Power Clean Original 1L for Power Clean Lemon 1L).
3. **Same category and use, different format.** For example HE liquid detergent instead of pods. Say clearly that the format is different.
4. **No acceptable substitute.** Some SKUs have no equivalent in the Marvel range (glass cleaner, rinse aid, stain remover, bleach). Do not force a product that does a different job; restore stock instead.

Always:

- Prefer a substitute that is **in stock in the same store today** and, when there is a choice, an **MSL** SKU.
- Stay **within the same category**. Never offer a product from another category as a substitute.
- **Never suggest combining products** to replace a missing one, and never present bleach as an alternative to a cleaner (see PUB-03).
- Do not transfer claims. A substitute does not inherit the missing product's claims (for example "antibacterial" or "with aloe").
- Show the price difference honestly. The comparisons below use the trade list price and the list price per fl oz or per count from MCPG-01.

## 7 What to tell the store and the shopper

- Tell the store owner which SKU is missing, which substitute to put forward, and when stock is expected (see section 8).
- Move the substitute next to the empty facing only if the planogram allows it. Do not leave the gap unlabelled.
- For a format or scent change, explain the difference in one sentence. For a pack-size change, explain the price per fl oz or per count.
- If there is no acceptable substitute, say so and give the expected restock date rather than offering an unsuitable product.

## 8 Fix the cause, not only the shelf

A substitution is a stop-gap. Research on shopper behaviour shows that many shoppers do not substitute at all. In one field study of real stock-outs, 41.5% went to another store and 22% postponed the purchase (PUB-01). In Marvel's own data, on days when a SKU is out of stock, its first-choice substitute sells only 2% to 4% more than usual in the same store *(data)*. So most of the lost sale is not recovered on the shelf.

To fix the cause:

- **Reorder early enough.** Orders arrive a median of 2 days after ordering for Distributors, 3 days for Modern Trade and 5 days for Traditional Trade *(data)*. An order placed when the shelf is already empty means days without stock.
- **Prioritise small accounts.** Tier 3 accounts run out of stock 15.1% of the time against 2.9% for Tier 1 *(data)*. They also tend to order when the shelf holds only about four days of sales (MCPG-04).
- **Watch MSL SKUs first.** A store fails MSL compliance for the day if even one MSL SKU is at zero (MCPG-06, MCPG-08).
