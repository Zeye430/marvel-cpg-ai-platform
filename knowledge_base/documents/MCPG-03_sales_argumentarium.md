# Marvel Sales Argumentarium

> Marvel CPG is a fictional company created for the University at Buffalo CDA 650 capstone. This document was written by Team 1 for the project's RAG knowledge base. Figures marked *data* come from the project dataset; everything else is fictional business content.

## 1 How to use this argumentarium

This document gives field representatives the selling stories for Marvel's categories, the degreaser line, and each channel. Every argument is built on a fact the rep can show: a price from the product master, or a figure from the project dataset (marked *data*). Use it together with the objection-handling guide (MCPG-04) and the product sheets (MCPG-01).

Three rules for every conversation:

1. **Lead with the store's own business**: availability, sales and margin on its shelf.
2. **Quote only figures you can trace** to this document or the dashboard. If a number is not here, do not invent it.
3. **Never promise an execution uplift figure.** In Marvel's data, secondary displays, planogram compliance and shelf share show at most about a 1% sales difference for the same store and SKU. That is too small to promise. Sell execution on visibility and on what the account receives in return (MCPG-05), not on a guaranteed sales increase.

## 2 The Marvel story in one minute

- **A complete household-cleaning range from one supplier**: 23 SKUs across Surface Cleaners, Dishwashing and Laundry, under six brands.
- **Shoppers buy it.** *(data)* In the 17 months to May 2026, Marvel products generated about $70.6 million of consumer sales value in the 221 accounts. Laundry was 40% of that value, Surface Cleaners 32% and Dishwashing 28%.
- **The retailer earns a healthy margin.** At the reference consumer price, a retailer buying at list price keeps 23% to 28% of the shelf price before any trade discount (MCPG-01, section 5).
- **Availability is the biggest lever.** *(data)* Small accounts are out of stock far more often: 15.1% of store-days in Tier 3, against 2.9% in Tier 1. Fixing availability is the fastest way to grow sales on the same shelf.

## 3 Facts that work in every category

### 3.1 Availability sells

*(data)* Over January 2025 to May 2026, an estimated 4.8% of potential sales were lost to empty shelves, valued at list price at about $2.7 million. The loss is concentrated in small accounts: about 2.5% of potential sales in Tier 1, 6.5% in Tier 2 and 13.8% in Tier 3 (Team 1 V3 EDA, section 8.1). Stock-outs also last: the median stock-out runs 4 days, and one in ten lasts more than 17 days.

### 3.2 Promotions bring extra units

*(data)* On promotion days a store-SKU sells on average 12.9% more units than on normal days in the same month, at an average discount of 11%. By promotion type: BOGO +23%, Volume deals +20%, TPR (temporary price reduction) +15%, Feature +13%, Display +11%, Bundle +2%. Details and the calendar are in MCPG-07.

### 3.3 Weekends and seasons

*(data)* Shoppers buy about 22% more on Saturdays and Sundays than on weekdays, in every channel, so the shelf must be full by Friday. Each category has its own season:

- **Surface Cleaners:** spring cleaning. March and April run 11% to 18% above the average month, and July and August about 15% below.
- **Laundry:** higher in January and September, lowest in July.
- **Dishwashing:** the steadiest category, with a high point in November and December.

(Seasonal indexes from non-promotion days, Team 1 V3 EDA section 6.2.)

## 4 Category selling stories

### 4.1 Surface Cleaners: "one range for every surface job"

- Lead with the **Power Clean 1 L** pair (Lemon and Original), both MSL. They are interchangeable, which makes them easy to keep in stock (MCPG-02).
- Add the **Heavy-Duty Degreaser** for kitchens and workshops, the **Glass & Surface Spray** for shine, and **Multi-Surface Wipes** for convenience.
- Time the shelf to the spring peak: stock up for March and April and for the Spring Clean Blitz TPR (12% off Surface Cleaners in Modern and Traditional Trade, 3 to 23 March in both 2025 and 2026; MCPG-07).
- *(data)* The Degreaser 1 L is the #1 Surface Cleaner by consumer sales value (18.9% of the category), ahead of the Multi-Surface Wipes (15.6%) and Power Clean Lemon (14.5%).

### 4.2 Dishwashing: "the steady earner"

- The **Dishwasher Pods 45ct** is the #2 SKU in the whole portfolio by value and 30% of Dishwashing value *(data)*. Make sure it never runs out.
- The three 28 oz hand dish soaps (Citrus, Original, Antibacterial) are all MSL and substitute for each other (MCPG-02).
- Demand is steady all year, so Dishwashing is the easiest category to plan. Use the Holiday Bundle (November) and the Traditional Trade Support feature (April) for extra visibility.

### 4.3 Laundry: "the biggest basket"

- Laundry is the largest category (40% of consumer sales value) and holds two of the top three SKUs: **Laundry Pods 42ct** (#1 in the portfolio) and **Laundry Detergent HE 100oz** (#3) *(data)*.
- Offer both formats: liquid for value per load and pods for convenience. Keep the smaller packs (Pods 20ct, HE 50oz) as entry options.
- Push stock ahead of the January and September peaks and the October Traditional Trade Support TPR (8% off Laundry).

## 5 The Marvel Pro Heavy-Duty Degreaser line

The degreaser line is two SKUs: the **Heavy-Duty Degreaser 1L** (SKU-1004, MSL) and the **Heavy-Duty Degreaser 500ml** (SKU-1005, optional). It is the heavy-duty option in Surface Cleaners for grease and baked-on grime on stovetops, range hoods, oven exteriors and grills (MCPG-01, sections 2.4 and 2.5).

**Why a store should carry it** *(data unless stated)*:

1. **It is already the category leader.** The 1 L is the #1 Surface Cleaner by consumer sales value (18.9% of the category, #4 of 23 in the whole portfolio).
2. **It earns the most per unit sold.** At $5.80 list and $8.07 reference shelf price, the 1 L is the highest-priced Surface Cleaner. Each unit sold brings the store more value than any other product in the category. At the reference shelf price the retailer keeps about 28% before trade discounts.
3. **The 500 ml sells as fast as the 1 L.** Where it is stocked, the 500 ml sells about the same number of units per in-stock day as the 1 L: about 9.8 vs 9.7 units a day in Tier 1 accounts, 4.7 vs 4.6 in Tier 2 and 1.9 in both in Tier 3. It is a low-price entry pack ($3.50 list) that does not slow the shelf.
4. **There is room to grow.** The 500 ml is carried by only 128 of 221 accounts, and by just 20 of the 65 Tier 3 accounts. The 1 L is MSL and should be in every account.
5. **It has a season.** Surface Cleaners peak in March and April, and the Spring Clean Blitz (12% off in March) covers the whole category in Modern and Traditional Trade.

**Suggested pitch by account type:**

- **Tier 1 and Modern Trade:** both sizes side by side. The 1 L for regular buyers and the 500 ml as a trial pack, ideally with a secondary display during the spring peak.
- **Tier 2 and Tier 3 / Traditional Trade:** start with the 1 L (it is MSL), add the 500 ml as one facing. Agree a reorder point so the line does not run out (MCPG-04, section 2.8).
- **Distributors:** include both sizes in the next volume order, timed to the Distributor Volume Deal periods (MCPG-07).

## 6 Channel stories

### 6.1 Modern Trade (supermarkets and chains)

- Talk category management: complete range, planogram compliance and visibility.
- Modern Trade has the strongest execution base *(data)*: planogram compliance of 95% in Tier 1 accounts, and the lowest stock-out rate of the three channels (6.7%).
- Use the promotion calendar (Spring Clean Blitz, Summer Refresh, Back to School, Fall Cleaning Event BOGO, Holiday Bundle, Modern Trade Exclusive) to plan displays and orders together.

### 6.2 Traditional Trade (neighbourhood and convenience stores)

- Talk availability and simplicity: the MSL SKUs first, then one or two extra facings.
- Deliveries take longer and are less punctual in this channel *(data)*: a median 5 days from order to arrival, and 24% of deliveries arrive late. Help the store order before the shelf is empty.
- Traditional Trade is invoiced about every two weeks, so compare orders with sales over the same two-week window (MCPG-08).

### 6.3 Distributors (wholesale accounts)

- Distributors are wholesale accounts that resell to smaller stores. Talk volume, assortment breadth and the volume-deal calendar, not shelf execution.
- Volume deals give 14% off with a minimum of 200 units, in two windows a year (Q1: about 20 January to 2 March; Q3 2025: 14 July to 24 August). Trade discount limits are in MCPG-05.

## 7 What not to claim

- **No execution uplift numbers.** Do not tell an account that a display, a planogram fix or extra shelf share will raise sales by a set percentage. The data does not support a figure larger than about 1% (Team 1 V3 EDA, section 8.7).
- **No competitor comparisons.** The project has no competitor data, so do not quote competitor prices, shares or performance.
- **No product claims beyond the label.** Do not add germ-kill rates, "safe for all surfaces" or medical claims (MCPG-01, PUB-03).
- **No approvals.** A rep cannot approve a discount above the account's cap. See MCPG-05 for what needs manager review.

**Open points and review notes**

- The client brief calls it 'the new degreaser line'. In the data, both degreaser SKUs sell from January 2025, so 'new' here means new to an account's shelf.
- The client described a built-in execution uplift (display +8%, planogram +5%, shelf share +3% per 10 points). The data shows at most about 1%, so this guide does not use those figures until the difference is explained.
