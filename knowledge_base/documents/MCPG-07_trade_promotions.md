# Trade Promotions Guide and 2025-2026 Promotion Calendar

> Marvel CPG is a fictional company created for the University at Buffalo CDA 650 capstone. This document was written by Team 1 for the project's RAG knowledge base. Figures marked *data* come from the project dataset; everything else is fictional business content.

## 1 Purpose

This guide describes Marvel's consumer promotions: the promotion types, the 2025 and 2026 calendar, what promotions delivered in the data, and how a representative prepares a store for one. Promotions are planned by Marvel for a whole channel and category. During a promotion, **every account** in that channel sells the promoted category at the promoted price.

## 2 Promotion types and the sales lift each delivers

**BOGO promotions give the biggest sales lift (+23% units), followed by Distributor Volume deals (+20%) and TPRs (+15%). Bundles add almost nothing (+2%).**

*(data)* "Extra units" compares, for the same store and SKU in the same month, average daily units on promotion days with non-promotion days, using in-stock days only. The implied elasticity is the percentage change in units divided by the percentage change in price.

| Type | What it is | Average discount | Extra units vs normal days *(data)* | Implied price elasticity *(data)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BOGO | Buy One Get One: a multi-buy mechanic. In the calendar it is valued as a discount percentage. | 15% | +23% | -1.29 |
| Volume | Distributor volume deal: a discount for orders above a minimum quantity. | 14% | +20% | -1.20 |
| TPR | Temporary Price Reduction: a straight price cut for a fixed period. | 10% | +15% | -1.27 |
| Feature | The product is featured in the retailer's advertising (flyer, digital or in-store media) at a reduced price. | 8% | +13% | -1.38 |
| Display | The promoted product gets an off-shelf display (endcap, island) together with a price reduction. | 9% | +11% | -1.18 |
| Bundle | Products sold together as a bundle at a combined price, for example around the holidays. | 12% | +2% | -0.18 |

Across all promotions, a store-SKU sold **12.9% more units at an average discount of 11%**, an elasticity of about **-1.0**: units rise by roughly the same percentage as the price falls. By channel *(data)*: Modern Trade +12.2% (elasticity -1.02), Traditional Trade +11.0% (-0.96), Distributors +19.9% (-1.20). By category: Surface Cleaners +12.6%, Dishwashing +11.7%, Laundry +15.2%.

Bundles are the exception: almost no extra units, so use them for visibility and basket-building, not for volume.

## 4 Preparing a store for a promotion

1. **Order early.** Stock-outs are more frequent on promotion days *(data)*: Modern Trade 8.3% vs 6.3% on other days, Traditional Trade 10.4% vs 8.6%, Distributors 10.1% vs 7.7%. Place the order so it arrives before the start date: delivery takes a median of 2 days for Distributors, 3 for Modern Trade and 5 for Traditional Trade.
2. **Secure the space.** For Display and Feature promotions, agree the display location and the price communication with the store.
3. **Check MSL first.** A promotion on a category does not help if an MSL SKU in that category is out of stock.
4. **Watch the end.** After a deep promotion, the store may hold extra stock; adjust the next order instead of pushing more volume.

## 5 Promotions and trade discounts are different

- A **consumer promotion** lowers the price shoppers pay for a planned period. It appears in the calendar above.
- A **trade discount** lowers the price the account pays Marvel on an invoice. Its limits are the guardrails in MCPG-05.

Never add the two together into one discount, and never present a promotion as a substitute for a guardrail approval.

## 6 Minimum volume

Each promotion has a `min_volume_units` value (20 to 200 units). Distributor Volume Deals require at least 200 units. Whether the minimum applies per invoice line, per order or over the whole promotion period has not been confirmed. In the data, only about a third of promoted invoice lines reach the minimum on their own.
